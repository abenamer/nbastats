Model comparison

Two readings of the same forecasts. Series: every played series is one trial, scored on the model's probability for its two actual participants with the series' actual home court (two-sided Brier, coin flip = 0.25; log loss, coin flip = 0.693; whether the favourite won). Fifteen trials a season instead of one, so the standard errors are small enough to tell models apart; the paired difference against the first model removes the variation the series share. Champion: Brier over the 16 playoff teams per season (uniform 1/16 forecast = 0.9375) and −ln of the probability given to the actual champion. Standard errors are across series or seasons; the forecasts are exact, so this is uncertainty about calibration, not Monte Carlo error. "elo_tuned_rolling" picks the home-court bonus and Elo scale that minimise the series log loss on earlier seasons only, then forecasts the next season, so tuned parameters are never scored on the seasons that chose them.

Assumptions and limitations

  • Inputs are FiveThirtyEight-style Elo ratings taken immediately before each postseason (after the play-in where it exists); ratings are held fixed through a simulated playoff run unless a K-factor is set.
  • Elo ratings are imported, never invented: seasons without complete playoff records are excluded from backtests and listed as skipped.
  • Home court in hypothetical matchups goes to the better regular-season record (then the better seed); real series use their actual game-1 host. Series formats follow the era (best-of-five first rounds before 2003, 2-3-2 Finals 1985–2013, neutral floor in 2020).
  • Baselines: equal (every game 50/50), win % (log5 through the same bracket), seeding (better seed wins a series with fixed probability 0.75).
  • Injuries, trades, rest and matchup effects are not modelled; franchise Elo measures team strength and cannot attribute outcomes to any single player.
