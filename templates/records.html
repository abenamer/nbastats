The highest and lowest Elo ratings in the game log, 1946-47 to today, or over the seasons of the eras picked below. A team's rating after each game is the measure; "peak" and "low" are the best and worst single-game ratings of a season, and "Reached in" names the phase of that game: regular season, play-in, cup final or playoffs. Reg. W-L is the regular-season record; Playoff W-L counts playoff games only. Conference and division records use the mean pre-playoff rating of the teams. Before 1970-71 the league's two divisions (Eastern and Western) stand in for conferences. ABA teams are not aligned.

By collective bargaining agreement

NBA/BAA seasons grouped by the agreement between the league and the players that was in force: the pre-cap years, the Robertson settlement, the first salary cap and each later agreement from the first season it applied to. For each period: the best and weakest team-season, the titles and who won them, the title HHI (the sum of each champion's squared share of the period's titles: 1.00 when one franchise won them all, lower when titles spread out), the mean Elo (the mean season-end rating across the league, averaged over the period's seasons: a check that the ratings do not drift between periods) and the Elo spread (the standard deviation of season-end ratings across the league, averaged the same way: wider means a more unequal league). A change at a period boundary also coincides with expansion and rule changes, so read these as association, not cause. The periods and their changes are curated from the public record.

Roads to the title by era

A champion's road is the mean rating of its opponents before each game, over the regular season and the playoffs together (each phase weighs by its games). For each NBA/BAA era (the archive has no ABA champions), the champion with the hardest road and the one with the easiest; with one title in the era, both are the same.
