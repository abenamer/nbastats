The 100 players since 1976-77 with the largest career worth, in tiers. The worth has two sources. The box score of a player's career says what his statistics are worth to a team. The games say what happened: the gap, in Elo, between his teams' margins in the games he played and the games he missed, with each margin adjusted for the opponent and the home court. Neither source is enough alone: the box score does not see everything a player does, and the gap is a noisy number that is missing for a player who never sat. The page starts each player from his box score and lets his games move the number, each weighted by how sure it is. The ranking is then drawn many times, and a tier is the smallest group a player is more likely than not to belong to. The list is computed from the stored games and box scores, and computed again after each data pull.

How it is computed

The games. The regular-season games from 1976-77 with a box score for the team. A stint is one player with one team in one season, with at least 5 games played and 24 minutes a game, so a reserve's games on the bench are not read as absences. A stint runs from his first game for the team to his last: the team's games before and after are not absences, so a season a team ends without him (a trade, a shutdown, an injury that ends the season) adds nothing. A margin is the team's margin plus the opponent's pre-game Elo above 1500, less the home-court bonus of 100 Elo, at 28 Elo per point; the player's own team's rating is not used, because it adapts while he is out. Inside a stint the gap is the mean adjusted margin of the games he played less that of the games he missed. The career gap pools the stints, each weighted by its effective number of games (played times missed over their sum), and its error comes from the spread of the margins inside the stints.

Declared absences. The stored data has no rosters of past seasons, so the stint rule cannot see a season a player sat out in whole, or the games before his first game and after his last. Such seasons are declared by hand: the seasons in the decision record. A season is declared when one injury, one illness or one retirement that he came back from kept him out of at least half of his team's games, with the same team before and after. The team's games of that season are then read as games he missed, inside his stint of that season. When he has no stint in that season, they are read inside his stint of the season before, or of the season after. A season read against another season counts for less, because a team changes between two seasons by about 3.1 points a game: a whole season of 82 games counts as about 14 games. The Note column names a declared season. A suspension, a holdout, a season in another league, and an outage in which the team traded or released him are not declared. Each declaration is a judgement about history, and after a long injury to a leading player a team often changes itself, so such a gap can be too large.

The box score. The career box worth is the box-score worth of each of his seasons (the Players page), weighted by the minutes. A line is fitted over every ranked player with a gap: the gap the games show against the career box worth. The line and the spread of the true gaps around it give each player a starting value and its error. The spread is what the box score does not see.

The worth and the ranking. The worth is the starting value and the gap together, each weighted by its precision. Many absences pull the worth toward the gap; few leave it near the box score. A player with 400 games as a regular is ranked. Each draw adds to every worth a normal error of its own size and ranks the players; the chances are the shares of the draws that put a player in the best 10, 25, 50 and 100, and the expected rank is his mean place. The list is ordered by tier, then by expected rank, and holds the first 100. The draws use a fixed seed, so the same data gives the same list. Active players only keeps the players the last roster refresh listed on a team, on the list and below it; a place and a tier stay those among every ranked player.

Limits. The list leans on the box score where a player missed few games, and the box score undervalues defence and what does not reach a statistic. An absence is not an experiment: a player misses games when he is hurt, a team often rests others at the same time, and the absences of team-mates are not separated. An absence at the start or the end of a season is not read unless it is declared, and a declaration needs half of a season out, so a shorter one stays unread. The gap is against the players who replaced him on that team, so a deep team shows less for the same player. The playoffs and the seasons before 1976-77 are not read, so a career that started earlier is read from 1976-77. The half-career check on the cards builds the worth on half of a player's seasons and compares it with the games of the other half. A place on the list is a statement of chance, among the ranked players only.
