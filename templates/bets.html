A pot staked on every game the sportsbook priced, read as a stock portfolio: start with a pot on a date, bet each game, and watch the pot. Five strategies run side by side. Three put the same stake on a favourite: the sportsbook's, the model's, and the model's only where it differs from the sportsbook's. Two size the stake by how far the model's chance sits from the sportsbook's and bet the side the model rates higher: the stake grows with the gap, once in proportion and once as its square. Each can be read at the posted prices or at the fair ones, the sportsbook's prices with its margin taken out, so the margin's cost shows apart from the forecasts' worth. Nothing here is advice and nothing changes the model: it is the record the model's disagreements with the market have to earn, in money.

The pot

By season

Each season's games from the start date, at the prices chosen above. Won is the bets the strategy won and Net what it made or lost. Disagreements are the games where the model's favourite is not the sportsbook's, bet on the model's side; Right is how many of those the model's side won. For the two sized strategies, Bets counts the games the model leaned on either way and Staked the money laid over them.

How it is computed

The games are those the Model vs Market record has scored: a snapshot of the model's chance and the sportsbook's moneylines saved before the tip-off, matched to the result. The sportsbook's favourite is the side its margin-free chance puts at one half or above; the model's is the side its chance does. The model's lean is the side it rates above the sportsbook, and the gap is the difference in chance: with the gap for one stake at 5 points, a gap of 10 points lays twice the stake in proportion and four times as the square, and a game the two price alike is not bet. A win at the posted price returns the stake times the moneyline's payout (a $100 bet at −150 returns $66.67, at +130 returns $130); a loss costs the stake. At the fair price a win on a side the sportsbook gives chance p returns the stake times (1 − p) / p. The pot is the running sum, so a run never stops for want of money. Net is the final pot less the starting one; the standard error of a net is the spread of the bets' returns times the square root of their count, so a net within two errors of zero is what luck alone gives. Drawdown is the largest fall from a peak of the pot to a later value.

Caveats: for the seasons filled from the sportsbook's history the model's side is the stored Elo alone, with no absence priced, and the lines were read once a night, nearer an opening line than a closing one; the live rows carry the model with its absences priced and a line within minutes of the close. A game the sportsbook priced at exactly even counts as a home favourite. The sized stakes have no cap, so a few games with a wide gap can carry a run. The bets of one night share the injury report and the ratings, so the standard errors are a little small.
