# MIT Sloan Sports Analytics Conference (SSAC) papers on NBA game, series, season and championship prediction and simulation, 2007-2026

**Access note.** The papers and pages (the Sloan site and its PDF host, arXiv, the journal sites, ESPN, FiveThirtyEight) could not be opened in the research session; only web search worked. Every figure below therefore comes from search-result snippets of the primary page or of a secondary write-up, not from reading the PDF. Treat all numbers as **second-hand until verified against the PDF**; I mark the ones that come from a snippet of the paper's own abstract page as "(abstract snippet)" and the ones that come from press or citing papers as "(secondary)". I did not find, and therefore do not report, any Sloan NBA paper that publishes a game-outcome accuracy, log loss or Brier score against a betting-line baseline; that absence is itself a finding (see Gaps).

## Key question 1: Which Sloan papers address NBA game/series prediction, win probability, season simulation, playoff/championship probabilities or team-strength estimation (including competition finalists and winners)?

### Takeaway
Across 2007-2026 the Sloan research track has featured very few papers whose primary deliverable is an NBA game, series or season forecast; the closest are Oh, Keshri and Iyengar's 2015 "Graphical Model for Basketball Match Simulation" (possession/touch-level simulation), Sandholtz and Bornn's 2018 "Replaying the NBA" (Markov-decision-process possession simulation used for counterfactual seasons), Wolfson and Koopmeiners' "Who's good this year?" (cross-sport predictability of game outcomes, NBA the most predictable), and a lineage of player/lineup rating papers evaluated by out-of-sample game prediction (Sill 2010 RAPM; Gong and Chen 2023 positional plus-minus; Jenkins 2026 deep-RL valuation on a win-probability model). Most Sloan basketball finalists instead concern tracking-data description (rebounding, defense, shot quality, play sketching), draft and roster forecasting, or strategy.

### Cited Findings

**Inventory of Sloan basketball papers relevant to prediction, simulation or team strength (chronological)**

- **2010 - Joseph Sill, "Improved NBA Adjusted +/- Using Regularization and Out-of-Sample Testing."** Presented at SSAC on March 6, 2010. The paper's evaluation framework judges adjusted plus-minus (APM) models by how well they predict the outcomes of future games, and introduces ridge regression ("RAPM"), described as nearly doubling APM's accuracy. — [PDF, text seen at a course-site mirror](https://supermariogiacomazzo.github.io/STOR538_WEBSITE/Articles/Basketball/Basketball_Sill.pdf) (abstract snippet); won the prize for best non-academic paper at SSAC 2010 — [PLOS ONE (secondary)](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0237920); listed as the origin of RAPM in [Harvard Data Science Review (secondary)](https://hdsr.mitpress.mit.edu/pub/kxks56er/download/pdf).
- **2010 - Huizinga and Weil, value of blocked shots** (expected points from the initial conditions of a possession); descriptive, not an outcome forecaster — [Basketball-Reference blog recap of SSAC 2010 (secondary)](https://www.basketball-reference.com/blog/index7f04.html?p=4741).
- **2011 - Allan Maymin, Philip Maymin, Eugene Shen, "How Much Trouble Is Early Foul Trouble? Strategically Idling Resources in the NBA."** Uses NBA play-by-play data and a win-probability method (borrowed from financial economics / option value) to derive the "Q+1" rule for benching a starter in foul trouble; the authors say the win-probability method can be applied to other problems. — [Sloan paper page](https://sloansportsconference.com/research-papers/how-much-trouble-is-early-foul-trouble); journal version International Journal of Sport Finance 7(4), 324-339 (2012) — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/jsf/intjsf/v7y2012i4p324-339.html).
- **2012 - Robert Ayer, "Big 2's and Big 3's: Analyzing How a Team's Best Players Complement Each Other."** Regression of team wins on two- and three-player archetype combinations over more than 30 years of NBA data, controlling for player quality and coaching; reported a versatile three-point-shooting wing plus a high-scoring, high-rebounding center adds 7.59 wins per season, while two high-scoring wings cost 4.05 wins per season. — [ESPN TrueHoop (secondary)](https://africa.espn.com/blog/truehoop/post/_/id/38016/you-me-synergy-the-big-2-and-the-big-3); [InformationWeek (secondary)](https://informationweek.com/it-sectors/analytics-drives-next-generation-of-moneyball-in-sports). (Author's surname appears as "Ayer" and "Ayers" across sources.)
- **2012 (Sloan proceeding; arXiv 2015; JSA 2018) - Julian Wolfson, Joseph S. Koopmeiners (and Andrew DiLernia in the journal version), "Who's good this year? Comparing the information content of games in the four major US sports."** One citing source dates the Sloan proceeding to 2012; the arXiv preprint is 2015 and the Journal of Sports Analytics version is 2018. — [JSA 2018](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3233/JSA-170199); dating conflict noted in [Wills, "The competitiveness of games in professional sports leagues" (secondary)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/313234849_The_competitiveness_of_games_in_professional_sports_leagues). Results are in Key question 2.
- **2012 - Rajiv Maheswaran et al., "Deconstructing the Rebound with Optical Tracking Data"** (2012 competition winner) and "The Three Dimensions of Rebounding" (Maheswaran, Chang, Su, Kwok, Levy, Wexler, Hollingsworth). Tracking-data description, not outcome prediction. — [MIT News on SSAC 2012 (secondary)](https://news.mit.edu/2012/sloan-sports-analytics-conference-0306); [Sloan page](https://www.sloansportsconference.com/research-papers/the-three-dimensions-of-rebounding). Other 2012 basketball titles on the conference page: "CourtVision," "Effort vs. Concentration: The Asymmetric Impact of Pressure on NBA Performance," "From 5 to 13: Redefining the Positions in Basketball" — [SSAC 2012 page](https://sloansportsconference.com/conference/2012-conference).
- **2013 - Matt Goldman and Justin Rao, "Live by the Three, Die by the Three? The Price of Risk in the NBA."** Game-theoretic model of shot selection under win-maximization; four years of play-by-play; testable condition that 3-point success must fall relative to 2-point success as risk appetite rises; held only for trailing teams, leading teams became risk-averse "contrary to what their risk preferences ought to be to maximize the chance of winning the game." — [Sloan paper page](https://www.sloansportsconference.com/research-papers/live-by-the-three-die-by-the-three) (abstract snippet). Win probability is the objective function, not a published forecast.
- **2014 - Dan Cervone, Alex D'Amour, Luke Bornn, Kirk Goldsberry, "POINTWISE: Predicting Points and Valuing Decisions in Real Time with NBA Optical Tracking Data"** (expected possession value, EPV). Possession-level stochastic-process prediction from SportVU tracking; journal version "A Multiresolution Stochastic Process Model for Predicting Basketball Possession Outcomes." — [arXiv 1408.0777](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1408.0777). It predicts points within a possession, not game winners; I could not open the PDF to extract its evaluation numbers.
- **2014 - Andrew Bocskocsky, John Ezekowitz, Carolyn Stein, "The Hot Hand: A New Approach to an Old 'Fallacy'" / "Heat Check."** Tracking-data test of streak shooting; finds a hot hand once shot difficulty is controlled. — [Significance magazine (secondary)](https://significancemagazine.com/?p=166); [WBUR (secondary)](https://www.wbur.org/onlyagame/2014/05/24/basketball-hot-hand-harvard-research). Relevant only as evidence about within-game non-stationarity.
- **2015 - Min-hwan Oh, Suraj Keshri, Garud Iyengar (Columbia), "Graphical Model for Basketball Match Simulation."** Probabilistic graphical model of match progression; each node is a network structure of the players on court, their actions and events; each touch is a transition between discrete states; "changes in the team lineup or the opponent's lineup significantly affect the dynamics of a match"; evaluated on 2013-14 NBA data. — [Sloan paper page](https://www.sloansportsconference.com/research-papers/graphical-model-for-baskeball-match-simulation) (abstract snippet; the site misspells "Baskeball"); [PDF on Sloan's file host](https://global-uploads.webflow.com/5f1af76ed86d6771ad48324b/5fee0924973b9bd5f61eec25_1081_rppaper_graphical-model-baskeball_final_edition.pdf); [Semantic Scholar record](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Graphical-Model-for-Baskeball-Match-Simulation-Keshri-Iyengar/45a39be7c6467356f74f896ab21126014236bd25). Keshri's Columbia dissertation "Essays in Basketball Analytics" includes the work as a chapter and extends evaluation to the 2013-16 NBA seasons. — [Columbia Academic Commons](https://academiccommons.columbia.edu/doi/10.7916/d8-1ghx-zy51); [CORE PDF](https://files01.core.ac.uk/download/231878528.pdf). This is the only Sloan paper I found whose stated purpose is simulating NBA matches.
- **2015 competition context:** 189 abstracts submitted; judges split the $30,000 prize between Rosales and Spratt's called-strike paper and a Harvard group's NBA defense paper ("Counterpoints"). — [FiveThirtyEight (secondary)](https://fivethirtyeight.com/features/whats-happening-at-the-2015-mit-sloan-sports-analytics-conference). - **2016 basketball finalist:** "Recognizing and Analyzing Ball Screen Defense in the NBA" (eight finalists that year). — [ESPN (secondary)](https://www.espn.com/nba/story/_/id/14931971/mit-sloan-research-papers-reveal-analytics-new-frontier). - **2018 - Nathan Sandholtz and Luke Bornn, "Replaying the NBA,"** Proceedings of the 12th MIT SSAC, 2018, pp. 1-13. Models each possession as a non-stationary Markov decision process with shot-clock-dependent transitions estimated by Bayesian hierarchical models; lineup-specific MDPs are combined into team-average MDPs; the simulator runs at sub-second level and records every on-ball event; seasons are simulated on-policy and under altered shot policies to measure net changes in efficiency and production (case: 2015-16 Cleveland Cavaliers). — citation and year in [Erazo, "Smart Sports Predictions via Hybrid Simulation" (secondary)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.09918); expanded version [arXiv 1812.05170](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1812.05170) and Annals of Applied Statistics 14(3), 1122-1145 (2020) — [Project Euclid](https://projecteuclid.org/euclid.aoas/1600454859); [author PDF](https://www.lukebornn.com/papers/sandholtz_aoas_2020.pdf). - **2019 - Nathan Sandholtz, Jacob Mortensen, Luke Bornn, "Chuckers: Measuring Lineup Shot Distribution Optimality Using Spatial Allocative Efficiency Models."** Bayesian hierarchical model of FG% and attempt rate over the half court; "lineup points lost" (LPL) between observed and optimal allocation; Cavaliers starters compared with random allocation. — [Sloan paper page](https://www.sloansportsconference.com/research-papers/chuckers-measuring-lineup-shot-distribution-optimality-using-spatial-allocative-efficiency-models); [arXiv 1912.05129](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.05129). Lineup evaluation, not game forecasting.

### Inferences
- The Sloan basketball track rewards novel data (optical tracking from 2012, body pose, broadcast video, transformers) and decision analysis, so the "does this model beat Vegas on game winners" question has been left to journals (IJF, JQAS, AoAS, TAS) and to arXiv; a report should say Sloan is not where NBA forecast-accuracy benchmarks live.
- Team strength at Sloan is almost always estimated bottom-up from player or lineup ratings (APM/RAPM 2010, positional plus-minus 2023, deep-RL valuation 2026) and validated by out-of-sample game prediction, rather than by Elo-style team ratings.

- I found no evidence of a Sloan paper on NBA playoff-series probabilities or championship odds specifically; the Rafique-Sanders paper is the only playoff-simulation paper and it is cross-sport.
- Henry Wang, Arnab Sarker and Anette Hosoi, "The Effect of Basketball Analytics Investment on NBA Team Performance" (Journal of Sports Analytics, 2025) surfaced in a Sloan-related search but I could not confirm it was presented at Sloan. — [JSA](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/15270025251328264)

## Key question 2: What accuracy did each report, on what test set and metric?

### Takeaway
Only a handful of Sloan basketball papers publish forecast-accuracy numbers at all, and none that I could find publish game-winner accuracy, log loss or Brier score against a betting-market baseline; the concrete numbers available are Sill's test RMSE of 11.47 points (R-squared 0.169) for RAPM-based prediction of game margins, Wolfson-Koopmeiners' >65% NBA game-winner accuracy with an 87.5% training split, and Farrell et al.'s 63%/87% roster-success classification accuracy.

### Cited Findings
- **Sill (2010), RAPM:** evaluation by predicting outcomes of future games; test RMSE 11.47 (R-squared 0.169) for one model configuration; "the benefit of additional years of data and the use of a minutes cutoff turns out to be modest once regularization is applied." Ridge regression described as nearly doubling APM accuracy. — [PDF, text seen at a course-site mirror](https://supermariogiacomazzo.github.io/STOR538_WEBSITE/Articles/Basketball/Basketball_Sill.pdf) (snippet). No Vegas comparison surfaced in any snippet.
- **Wolfson, Koopmeiners, DiLernia ("Who's good this year?"):** the NBA was the most predictable of the four major leagues; their model reached over 65% accuracy across seasons using 87.5% of games for training; home teams won about 60% of NBA games, the largest home edge of the four leagues; the information metric levels off at around 25 games, and in the first 5-10 games each added game improves accuracy by roughly 1 percentage point. — [JSA 2018](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3233/JSA-170199) (snippet). A later Loyola paper reports an AUC of 0.756 for NBA games and says mid-season won-loss ratio and point differential are about as good as its strength estimates, "confirming the findings of the Wolfson group." — [Loyola eCommons (secondary)](https://ecommons.luc.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1025&context=math_facpubs).
- **Oh, Keshri, Iyengar (2015):** evaluated on 2013-14 NBA match data; conclusion is qualitative ("the graphical model approach is appropriate for modeling a basketball match"); no accuracy, RMSE, log loss or market comparison appeared in any snippet of the paper page, the Semantic Scholar record or the dissertation. — [Sloan paper page](https://www.sloansportsconference.com/research-papers/graphical-model-for-baskeball-match-simulation); [Semantic Scholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Graphical-Model-for-Baskeball-Match-Simulation-Keshri-Iyengar/45a39be7c6467356f74f896ab21126014236bd25).
- **Ayer (2012):** +7.59 wins/season for the best two-player archetype pairing; -4.05 wins/season for two high-scoring wings; regression over 30+ years controlling for player quality and coaching; no out-of-sample test reported in the snippets. — [InformationWeek (secondary)](https://informationweek.com/it-sectors/analytics-drives-next-generation-of-moneyball-in-sports).
- **Gong and Chen (2023):** "improved prediction accuracy in player plus-minus when factoring in player positions" over 2015-16 to 2021-22; magnitude and metric not in the abstract snippet. — [Sloan paper page](https://www.sloansportsconference.com/research-papers/estimating-positional-plus-minus-in-the-nba).
- **Farrell et al. (2025):** 63% accuracy predicting NBA roster success from psychological (interview-language) features alone; 87% when playing statistics and physical traits are added. — [Sloan paper page](https://www.sloansportsconference.com/research-papers/beyond-the-box-score-using-psychological-metrics-to-forecast-nba-success) (abstract snippet). This is a player-level classification, not game prediction.
- **Jenkins (2026):** claims "better predictive accuracy than RAPM" and more stability; no numbers in the abstract snippet. — [Sloan event page](https://www.sloansportsconference.com/event/deep-reinforcement-learning-for-nba-player-valuation-a-temporal-difference-approach-with-shapley-attribution).
- **Sandholtz and Bornn (2018/2020):** outputs are distributional estimates of counterfactual season production under altered shot policies, propagated through posterior samples; the search did not surface a forecast-accuracy metric. — [arXiv 1812.05170](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1812.05170); [Project Euclid](https://projecteuclid.org/euclid.aoas/1600454859).

**Non-Sloan benchmarks useful for calibrating expectations (clearly not Sloan papers):**
- Štrumbelj and Vračar (2012), International Journal of Forecasting 28(2), 532-542: possession-based homogeneous Markov simulation with transition matrix estimated from NBA play-by-play and from team summary statistics, compared with logit regression, a latent-strength rating and bookmaker odds; Markov forecasts were "comparable in quality" to the statistical methods while "bookmaker odds were the best probabilistic forecasts." — [IAOR abstract](https://iaorifors.com/paper/76013); [author listing](https://www.fri.uni-lj.si/en/node/1957).
- A 10-season NBA study found its approach had "similar performance as the odds from betting companies and does better than ELO." — [search summary pointing to sponet record "Predicting season outcomes for the NBA" (secondary, authors not captured)](https://www.sponet.de/sponet/Record/4078050?lng=en).
- Bayesian in-game NBA win probability (arXiv 2207.05114): TeamRankings.com pregame probabilities had a lower Brier score than Elo, with a small gap. — [arXiv 2207.05114](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.05114) (snippet).
- Dynamic Bradley-Terry (arXiv 2307.16642) performed comparably to FiveThirtyEight's public NBA Elo (Silver and Fischer-Baum 2015) and beat it in one bandwidth setting. — [arXiv 2307.16642](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.16642) (snippet).
- Yeh, Rice and Dubin, The American Statistician 76(3), 214-223 (2022): ESPN in-game NBA win probabilities are well-calibrated and beat naive baselines but show "no significantly improved skill over simple logistic regression models based on relative team strength and score difference"; Brier loss. — [arXiv 2010.00781](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2010.00781); [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/amstat/v76y2022i3p214-223.html).
- Typical ML game-winner accuracies: Lin et al. (2014) random forest 65.15% — [systematic review arXiv 2410.21484 (secondary)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.21484); best published NBA models bounded at roughly 66-72%, with one thesis model averaging 65.1% — [Bryant honors thesis](https://digitalcommons.bryant.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1000&context=honors_data_science). A 2025 PLOS One MLP claim of 98.90% should be treated with suspicion given this range — [PLOS One](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article/file?type=printable&id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0326326).
- Erazo (2023, Winter Simulation Conference), "Smart Sports Predictions via Hybrid Simulation: NBA Case Study": agent-based hybrid simulation; finds accuracy peaks within an optimal range of historical-data volume; no accuracy figure in the abstract. — [arXiv 2304.09918](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.09918).

### Inferences
- The ~65% game-winner accuracy that Wolfson-Koopmeiners report for the NBA is in the same band as the 65-72% ceiling reported in the broader literature, and the Štrumbelj-Vračar result that bookmakers beat a possession-level Markov simulator suggests richer simulation structure alone does not close the gap to the market.
- Sill's 11.47-point RMSE on margin is a usable reference point for a point-spread-error metric; Vegas lines are reported elsewhere to miss by roughly 9-10.5 points per game on average (non-peer-reviewed GitHub analysis — [NBA_Betting](https://github.com/NBA-Betting/NBA_Betting)), so a ~11.5 RMSE from a pure player-rating model would be worse than the market, but this comparison is my inference, not a published one.

### Gaps
- No Sloan NBA paper's exact test-set size, log loss, Brier score or calibration plot could be extracted because PDFs were unreachable; the Oh-Keshri-Iyengar PDF and Keshri dissertation are the top priority to read for a possession-simulation accuracy number.
- Whether Sill (2010) compared against Vegas spreads is unknown (no snippet mentioned it).
- POINTWISE/EPV evaluation metrics (2014) were not retrievable.

## Key question 3: What did the papers identify as limits of predictability (noise, injuries, rest, lineups) and what gains came from richer data (tracking, lineups) over team-level ratings?

### Takeaway
The Sloan literature's consistent message is that lineups matter (Oh-Keshri-Iyengar: lineup changes "significantly affect" match dynamics; Sandholtz-Bornn: lineup-specific MDPs; Ayer: archetype pairings worth several wins), that regularization rather than more data is what makes player ratings predictive (Sill), and that most of a season's information about team quality is in the first ~25 games (Wolfson-Koopmeiners); no Sloan basketball paper I found quantified injuries or rest as a forecast-error component.

### Cited Findings
- Lineups: "changes in the team lineup or the opponent's lineup significantly affect the dynamics of a match" (2013-14 data). — [Oh, Keshri, Iyengar, Sloan page](https://www.sloansportsconference.com/research-papers/graphical-model-for-baskeball-match-simulation) (abstract snippet).
- Lineups: lineup-specific MDPs combined into team-average MDPs via a transition weighting scheme; Bayesian hierarchical models borrow strength across players and time; uncertainty propagated via posterior samples. — [Sandholtz and Bornn, AoAS 2020 via arXiv 1812.05170](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1812.05170) (snippet).
- Lineup sparsity: an NBA team uses more than 600 lineups in a season, so the average lineup has about 25-30 possessions. — [Petridis and Pelechrinis, L-RAPM, arXiv 2601.15000 (2026; not Sloan)](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.15000).
- Data volume vs regularization: once ridge regularization is applied, extra years of data and minutes cutoffs give only modest gains. — [Sill 2010](https://supermariogiacomazzo.github.io/STOR538_WEBSITE/Articles/Basketball/Basketball_Sill.pdf) (snippet).
- Information content: NBA game outcomes carry the most information per game among the four leagues; information levels off around 25 games; ~1 point of accuracy per game for games 5-10. — [Wolfson et al., JSA 2018](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3233/JSA-170199) (snippet).
- Metric reliability: discrimination, stability and independence as "meta-metrics" for NBA statistics; stability is the fraction of a metric's variance due to true skill change rather than chance. — [Franks et al., arXiv 1609.09830](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/1609.09830).
- Within-game non-stationarity: a hot hand exists once shot difficulty is accounted for (2014). — [WBUR (secondary)](https://www.wbur.org/onlyagame/2014/05/24/basketball-hot-hand-harvard-research).
- Risk behaviour: leading teams tighten up contrary to win-maximization, trailing teams take optimal risk and show an efficiency boost. — [Goldman and Rao, Sloan page](https://www.sloansportsconference.com/research-papers/live-by-the-three-die-by-the-three).
- Positions: including player positions improves prediction of player plus-minus; versatile offensive players and paint-defending bigs are the most valuable contributors. — [Gong and Chen, Sloan page](https://www.sloansportsconference.com/research-papers/estimating-positional-plus-minus-in-the-nba).
- Cross-sport randomness (not Sloan): the NBA shows the largest dispersion in team talent and the largest home advantage, while NHL and MLB outcomes are the most random; single games reveal little because "team i will typically only play team j once at time k." — [Lopez, Matthews, Baumer, AoAS 2018 / arXiv 1701.05976](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1701.05976).
- Rest (not Sloan): back-to-back games show a win rate of about 44%, lower than expected independent of rest time, while road-trip order shows little effect — [arXiv 2606.27957](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.27957); Bowman, Harmon and Ashman (JSA 2023) find a visiting team on a back-to-back against a rested home team shifts win probability over 2000-01 to 2018-19 — [JSA](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/reader/10.3233/JSA-220629).

### Inferences
- For an Elo-style team-rating Monte Carlo, the Sloan evidence points to lineup availability as the single most-cited omitted variable; the "25 games" result implies season-long Elo updating adds little discriminating information after the first third of the season, so the gap to richer models is driven by roster/lineup state, not by more games of history.

### Gaps
- No Sloan paper quantified injuries, rest or schedule as a forecast-error component; the rest effects above are from journals and arXiv.
- No Sloan paper reported a head-to-head gain of tracking-based or lineup-based forecasts over a team-level Elo on game winners; the only tracking-vs-team comparison found is the non-Sloan Štrumbelj-Vračar result that a possession Markov simulator did not beat bookmakers.

## Key question 4: Which papers compared a Monte Carlo season/playoff simulation against closed-form or market baselines?

### Takeaway
Within Sloan, only Rafique and Sanders' tournament-design paper explicitly pairs closed-form probability derivations with Monte Carlo simulation (Bradley-Terry / Thurstone-Mosteller strengths), and it is cross-sport with the NBA mentioned only via the Play-In; Sandholtz-Bornn simulate NBA seasons but compare altered policies against the on-policy simulation, not against a market; the market-baseline comparison for possession-level simulation exists only outside Sloan (Štrumbelj-Vračar 2012, where bookmakers won).

### Cited Findings
- Rafique and Sanders: "closed-form theoretical probability derivations with Monte Carlo simulation experiments," team strength via Bradley-Terry and Thurstone-Mosteller; Page Playoff System raises the probability the strongest team wins while keeping ordinal fairness; NBA Play-In cited as an example of the hybrid design. — [Sloan paper page](https://www.sloansportsconference.com/research-papers/modern-playoff-tournament-design-analysis-across-sports-a-bayesian-modeling-and-advanced-simulation-study) (abstract snippet).
- Sandholtz and Bornn: after calibration, seasons simulated on-policy and under altered shot policies; baseline is the on-policy simulation, not a market. — [arXiv 1812.05170](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1812.05170).
- Oh, Keshri, Iyengar: match-level simulation evaluated on 2013-14 data; no market baseline mentioned in any snippet. — [Sloan paper page](https://www.sloansportsconference.com/research-papers/graphical-model-for-baskeball-match-simulation).
- Non-Sloan: Štrumbelj and Vračar's possession Markov simulation vs logit, latent strength and bookmaker odds; bookmakers best. — [IAOR](https://iaorifors.com/paper/76013).
- Non-Sloan: Lopez, Matthews, Baumer build Bayesian state-space strengths from betting-market data and propose competitiveness metrics for regular seasons and postseason tournament formats. — [arXiv 1701.05976](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1701.05976).
- Non-Sloan closed-form series work: Rump (2006) home-away Bernoulli model preferred to the binomial for best-of-seven series length — [BGSU ScholarWorks](https://scholarworks.bgsu.edu/asor_pub/2); a JSA paper finds constant series-win-probability models inconsistent with historical NBA Finals data — [JSA 10.3233/JSA-200422](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.3233/JSA-200422).

### Inferences
- There is no Sloan precedent for benchmarking an NBA Monte Carlo season/playoff simulation against market title odds; a report comparing an Elo Monte Carlo with Sloan work should state that the relevant benchmark literature is journal-based (IJF 2012, AoAS 2018, TAS 2022), and that the market (bookmakers / TeamRankings) consistently edges Elo and Markov simulators by small Brier margins in those sources.

### Gaps
- Year and full results (number of simulations, strength parameters, NBA-specific outputs) of Rafique-Sanders could not be confirmed.
- No Sloan paper on NBA championship probabilities, preseason season simulation, or playoff-bracket odds was found for any year 2007-2026; the 2025 and 2026 paper lists were partially visible (titles only) and nothing in them addresses game or season forecasting apart from the Jenkins win-probability-based player valuation.
