# Changelog

## 0.1.0 — 2026-10-21

- Backtest: series scored one by one. Every played series is a trial, scored on the model's probability for its two actual participants with the series' actual home court (two-sided Brier, log loss, whether the favourite won), overall and by round, with the paired difference against the first model of the run; the favourite's calibration joins the calibration panel, the season table carries the series totals, and the rolling tuning minimises the series log loss (`tuning.criterion`, default `series`; `champion` keeps the old criterion); a model with a K-factor, which has no exact probabilities, is refused instead of failing the job. With 315 series instead of 21 champions the models separate: the log5 model on regular-season record beats Elo by 0.037 in log loss (two standard errors of the paired difference), Elo is under-confident in the first round and over-confident in the conference finals, and rolling tuning gains nothing. Model version `backtest-1.1`; results stored before it show no series tables.
- Season-aware home-court bonus. The home advantage of the real games fell from about 85 Elo points in the 2000s to about 40 in the 2020s, so the forecasts of regular-season games now use a bonus fitted on the played regular-season games of the previous five seasons (at least 2,000 games, else 100), which lowers their out-of-sample log loss; playoff games and the rating updates keep the archive's 100, so the archive replay still holds. `SeasonParams` gains `regular_home_advantage` (filled with the fitted value when absent); new `GET /api/seasons/{season}/home-advantage`; the Season Forecast page shows the fitted value as the default of a new regular-season field and labels the old field as the playoff bonus; the question box's probability calculator prices a game with the current season's value. Fit in `domain::elo::fit_home_advantage`, window in `application::home_advantage`.
- Alpha Simulator: the question in reverse. A "Work backwards from the titles" panel turns a record (titles won in a window of seasons among a league's franchises) into the strength it implies, computed exactly: the title chance per season with its 95 percent range, the implied alpha against every other franchise at 1 with the range the record leaves open, the chance of the record at that strength and for an ordinary franchise, and the alpha at which the record becomes a coin flip; and, because an alpha is only a ratio of title odds, the same record as a strength: the Elo gap above the league mean a team of constant strength would have needed against the actual pre-playoff fields of the window's seasons, read as Elo points, points per game and wins per 82, beside what the franchise actually entered the playoffs at. A button puts the implied alpha on the franchise of interest. The page explains that the window drives the answer (four titles in eight seasons imply alpha 29, in 22 seasons 6.4). New `GET /api/strength`; `domain::simulation::exact::strength_for_titles` and the new pure module `domain::strength` over the playoff bracket engine.
- Alpha Simulator: the page explains what the lottery answers, what each input means and how to read each result, and a **Franchise of interest** control picks the franchise whose own dynasty probability is reported (it was always the first in the list). The concept [Alpha lottery engine](docs/concepts/alpha-engine.md) gains the purpose, the reading guide and the limits; the new runbook [Run an alpha scenario](docs/runbooks/alpha-scenario.md) walks through three questions.
- Records page: faceted search. The era chips are facets now: a pick keeps the seasons of that bargaining period, rule period, league size, league, decade, competition format, dynasty era, player era or themed era, and every table on the page is computed again over them (the game-level tables are ranked within the selected seasons by the store). Within one dimension the picks add up, across dimensions they intersect; a banner names the picks, the seasons and what changed; the address follows the picks. New dimension: themed era (the Reddit NBA era from 2008-09, the three-point dominance era from 2015-16), curated in `domain::eras`. The group table stays as a collapsed "Compare groups" panel computed within the same facets. Every bargaining-period row and group row, and the banner, shows the mean season-end Elo beside the spread, so a drift of the ratings between periods would show. `GET /api/records` takes repeated `facet` parameters (a malformed one answers 400) and answers `scope`; new `GET /api/records/facets`; `GET /api/records/groups` takes `facet` too and keeps the last value of a scalar parameter given twice. Dynasty facets use the default thresholds everywhere. The `AnalysisStore` game queries take a season scope.
- Game reports: every game score on the Records page links to the game's report (the Associated Press recap that ESPN hosts) when one is found. The page asks `GET /api/reports` for the dates it shows; the application looks a date up once in ESPN's public scoreboard feed, matches the games by team, keeps `https` ESPN links only and records the lookup. Ports `ReportStore` and `ReportSource`, adapter `EspnReportSource`, migration `0004_game_reports`, variables `GAME_REPORTS_ENABLED` and `ESPN_BASE_URL`.
- Records page: the era builder. Group the seasons by any combination of bargaining period, rule period, league size, league, decade, competition format (early playoffs, 16-team field, best-of-seven throughout, play-in era, NBA Cup era), dynasty era (computed from the champions with adjustable thresholds) and player era (curated careers of every league and Finals MVP, and George Mikan), with the records of each group, the dated changes of its periods, a compare view and a shareable link. New pure modules `domain::eras` and `domain::players`; `GET /api/records/groups`. The rule periods carry dated changes too.
- Records page: every peak and low line names the phase of the game that set it (regular season, play-in, cup final or playoffs), and the lists show the playoff record beside the regular-season record.
- Review notes resolved: an archive import and a results sync no longer run at the same time (the second answers 409); the question box reports "1 row" and answers unparseable tool arguments with an error the model can correct; the bargaining-period records serve owned strings and ignore a rating that is not a number.
- Records page: records by collective bargaining agreement. NBA/BAA seasons are grouped by the agreement in force (pre-cap, the Robertson settlement, the first salary cap and each agreement since), with the best and weakest team-season, the titles and their winners, the title HHI, the Elo spread and what each agreement changed. The period table lives in `domain::cba`, curated from the public record; the API's `/api/records` gains `by_cba`.
- Question box on the Dashboard: a plain-language question answered from the stored data through a language model (OpenRouter's OpenAI-compatible API, model and key by configuration) with seven read-only tools over the store ports; the answer lists its lookups. The box answers only questions about the stored data: the prompt forbids everything else and an answer without a lookup is replaced by a fixed refusal. Bounded per question (6 model calls, 10 tool calls, 75 seconds, 2 at a time). New port `LanguageModel`, adapter `OpenRouterModel`, routes `GET /api/ask/status` and `POST /api/ask`, variables `OPENROUTER_API_KEY`, `OPENROUTER_MODEL`, `OPENROUTER_BASE_URL`. Decided in ADR 0005.
- Configuration: `.env.example` lists every variable grouped by purpose with its default and empty secrets; `DATABASE_URL` is built by Compose from the `POSTGRES_*` values. Compose now passes `SIM_THREADS`, `BALLDONTLIE_BASE_URL` and `OPENROUTER_BASE_URL` into the container.
- Initial application: Engines A, B and C, backtesting, dynasty laboratory, nine pages, PostgreSQL 17 persistence, Docker Compose, backup and restore scripts.
- Aligned with REAS 1.2.0-draft: domain, application and adapters layout; six ports and one `PostgresStore` adapter; `TeamId`, `FranchiseId` and `SeasonYear` newtypes; typed errors; conditional job transitions; documentation checker, terminology, self-audit and decisions.

- CI: actions moved to releases that run on Node 24 (`actions/checkout@v5`, `actions/setup-python@v6`); the audit job runs a prebuilt `cargo audit` (via `taiki-e/install-action`) instead of `rustsec/audit-check`, which has no Node 24 release, so the deprecation warnings stop.
- Compute first: the application computes every Elo rating itself from 1946-47 on, with the archive's results and schedules as input and its ratings kept as reference values (`ref_` columns). Each game records its result source (archive, results, projection); archive games always stand. Start ratings follow the franchise lineage, with 1300 for a new franchise and the archive's seed for the clubs that joined from other leagues in 1948 to 1950. Migration `0003_compute_first`; the first start recomputes every season (about 15 seconds for 80 seasons); `nba-elo-simulator recompute` runs it by hand. Every write of ratings is one transaction, so a failure leaves the store as it was; a results sync re-rates the archive rows in place instead of writing them again. The daily sync tries the previous season once and stops when that season yields nothing. Decided in ADR 0004.
- Records page: eras are grouped into named rule periods (pre shot clock, shot clock, three-point line, zone defense, hand-check ban; the ABA is one period); a period start also starts an era, so the 29-team run splits at 2001-02. The era, clanker and road tables carry the period, and a legend lists the periods with their rule changes.
- Records page: every panel takes the full width and the table cells are tighter, so the tables no longer scroll sideways at common widths.
- Records page: the matchup and clanker tables name the winner and the margin of each game.
- Records page: the East-against-West table names the champion and its conference each season, green when the stronger conference won.
- Records page: the best and weakest champion lists show the rating after the clinching game next to the season peak; the best uncrowned teams show their season-end rating.
- Records page: the hardest and the easiest road to the title of each era (NBA/BAA; the archive has no ABA champions): a champion's mean opponent rating before each game over the regular season, over the playoffs, and over every game (each phase weighs by its games); the `AnalysisStore` port gains `champion_roads`. The best and worst champion lists now take champions from the championships table, so the curated seasons (1946-47 to 1959-60, 1967-68 to 1973-74) appear.
- Records page: greatest matchups, the 20 played games with the highest combined pre-game rating of the two teams, and clankers by era, the weakest game of each era with NBA/BAA and ABA apart (a league-wide list would only find the newest franchises, which start at 1300); the `AnalysisStore` port gains `matchups` and `weakest_matchup_per_season`.
- Elo Explorer: a click on a team code in the ratings table adds the team to the highlight field (a second click removes it) and reloads the chart; the table shows codes with the full name as a tooltip, so every column fits.
- Every table sorts on a header click (numbers as numbers, text as text, blanks last); a second click reverses the order.
- Static files carry a content hash in their URL and are served with `Cache-Control: no-cache`, so a rebuilt page never runs an old script with a new one. A chart whose creation fails no longer locks its canvas.
- Dependencies: tokio 1.54.1 (RUSTSEC-2026-0384, a race condition in `tokio::time::Interval` on 1.54.0, which was yanked).
- Results sync: the first sync of a season fetches the whole schedule; later syncs fetch only the days from the last played game to today and keep the stored games outside that window, so the daily refresh needs one or two requests instead of thirteen.
- Elo Explorer: the season chart shows a dot for a team with one game and a line from two games on, adds a shaded strip for the games not played yet (with their count and the last scheduled date), and keeps its date ticks on whole days.
- Curated champions and runner-ups for 1946-47 to 1959-60 and 1967-68 to 1982-83, where the archive's playoff data does not resolve a bracket; existing installations are backfilled at start.
- Conference and division alignment curated back to 1946-47 (NBA and BAA), so standings, the East-West comparison and the division records cover every season; existing installations are updated at start. The full-season rule for the group records follows each season's median game count instead of a fixed 60 games, so the 48-game and lockout seasons count.
- Records: a league's size per season comes from the schedule (played or not), so an in-progress season no longer appears as a smaller league of its own. The era table is split into NBA/BAA and ABA.

- Records page: best and worst by era, where an era starts when a league's team count changes and, for the NBA, at the 3-point line in 1979-80.
